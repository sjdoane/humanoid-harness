## Main point

Under its exact renderer, VLM, and trainer stack, a frozen CLIP image-text encoder with one goal prompt—and optionally a baseline-direction projection—supplies a per-state reward sufficient for SAC to solve 5 of 8 tested Humanoid tasks, but the reported failures under abstract rendering, subtle poses, and smaller VLMs make this evidence for a renderer-bound task-reward candidate, not a reference oracle or a reward-only result under Sam's frozen MDP.

## Mechanism

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    L[INPUT goal prompt l<br/>optional baseline b + alpha] --> TL[FROZEN CLIP text encoder]
    S[MDP state s] --> V[FROZEN renderer psi<br/>camera + textures + resolution]
    V --> VI[FROZEN CLIP image encoder]
    TL --> R[Cosine reward<br/>optional goal-baseline projection]
    VI --> R
    R --> O[OUTPUT scalar task reward R(s)]
    O --> B[Label replay-buffer transitions]
    B --> T[FROZEN DQN/SAC trainer<br/>hyperparameters + budget]
    T --> P[Task-specific policy]
    P -->|actions| E[FROZEN scene + observations<br/>actions + dynamics]
    E -->|next state feedback| S
    P --> Q[Independent ground-truth,<br/>EPIC, or human evaluation]
```

“Frozen” here is the transfer contract Sam would need. The paper itself changes textures and camera for an ablation, so those runs are different experiment families rather than reward-only comparisons under the project charter.

## What transfers to Sam's harness

- Treat a VLM reward as a versioned executable `task_reward_k` dependency bundle: exact VLM weight hash, tokenizer and image preprocessing, goal and baseline prompt bytes, `alpha`, reward formula, renderer/camera/textures/resolution, code commit, and batching/time-index semantics.
- Keep the scene, observation/action spaces, dynamics, renderer, foundation controller, tracking reward, trainer, budget, and evaluator fixed across reward candidates. The paper's texture/camera ablation is direct evidence that renderer changes can dominate reward quality.
- Use goal-baseline regularization as a narrow task-reward composition operator. It combines two language embeddings; it does not compose reference motions or create an oracle.
- Before policy training, sweep the candidate reward over held-out goal and non-goal states. Report separation, reward landscapes, and EPIC only on a declared state distribution.
- Keep objective task, safety, contact, stability, and naturalness metrics outside the VLM reward. The paper itself calls for independent monitoring because prompt-conditioned rewards can be misspecified.
- Route a human language edit only to declared reward fields such as the goal prompt, baseline prompt, or `alpha`. Record the predicted metric effect and mint a new immutable reward identity.
- Preserve the policy/reward observation distinction. In the release, rendered pixels feed CLIP while the task policy is an MLP over numeric simulator observations; adopting the reward does not make the policy vision-conditioned.

## What does not transfer

- The paper does not create, select, compose, crop, phase, or recover reference motions; it provides no closed-loop oracle, transition guards, or mode graph.
- A baseline prompt is not a reference baseline. It defines an embedding direction inside the reward model.
- The method configures a frozen neural scorer; it does not synthesize inspectable Python reward code with named physical terms, coefficients, or units.
- One prompt trains one task-specific policy. The experiments do not show inference-time language-conditioned policy steering or an iterative human-feedback loop.
- The image-only reward has no temporal input. Video rewards, multi-step task specification, and dialogue are future work.
- The paper does not support changing textures, camera, VLM scale, algorithm, or budget inside a reward-only comparison; each change crosses Sam's frozen boundary unless separately declared.
- Results do not establish real-robot transfer, robustness to adversarial optimization, safe deployment, or portability to Sam's controller and observation contract.
- No result implies that RL-Sculptor currently implements VLM-RM.

## Failure modes and caveats

- **Renderer distribution shift:** untextured MountainCar could not be learned; Humanoid kneeling rose from `36%` to `91%` after texture changes and to `100%` after also moving the camera.
- **Fine-pose discrimination:** hands on hips reached `64%`; arms crossed reached `0%`. The authors attribute these failures to subtle visual differences from ordinary standing.
- **Physics/reward confound:** standing on one leg reached `0%`; the authors note that round simulated feet may make the task hard or impossible, so this is not clean evidence of reward-model failure.
- **Scale threshold:** kneeling was `0%` with RN50, ViT-L-14, and ViT-H-14 but `100%` with ViT-bigG-14, despite smooth EPIC improvement. Reward-separation metrics did not identify the policy-learning threshold.
- **Regularization is not a rescue:** on the three failed Humanoid tasks, `alpha=0` and `0.2` were comparable and larger values degraded performance; the authors could not afford a fuller human-evaluation study.
- **Prompt misspecification:** a short description can omit intended behavior, and VLM generalization can be wrong. The paper says the risk grows with task complexity and recommends independent monitoring.
- **Evaluation is not fully independent:** one author labels trajectories; the final checkpoint is selected by highest training reward; the EPIC label dataset is collected from checkpoints of the highest-reward run using the largest VLM.
- **Threshold ambiguity:** the main text, Table 1, and Table 2 use “at least 50%,” while Appendix B's Figure 5 says “above 50 steps.” The treatment of the 50-step bucket is therefore not source-resolved.
- **Optimization robustness is untested:** robustness against reward hacking or policy optimization pressure is explicitly future work.
- **Release boundary:** the pinned code asserts CUDA availability, batches complete render sets into an off-policy replay buffer, and pins old library versions. It is evidence of a research implementation, not a portable or safety-hardened reward service.
